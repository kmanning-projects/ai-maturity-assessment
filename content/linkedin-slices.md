## Post 2 — The average lies
> A regional bank scored 2.8/5 on AI maturity. The number was useless. The shape told the whole story.

```
Data & Infra   ████████░░ 4
Talent         ██████░░░░ 3
Governance     ████░░░░░░ 2  ◀ constraint
Adoption       ████░░░░░░ 2
Strategy       ██████░░░░ 3
```

Their instinct? Buy more infrastructure — their *strongest* dimension. The actual problem: ungoverned pilots that can't reach production. Strong data feeding nothing.

The average hides the constraint. The profile shape reveals it.

## Post 3 — The binding-constraint rule
> Don't improve everything. Improve the one thing holding back everything else.

The rule I use:
• Raise your weakest *high-leverage* dimension by ONE level
• STOP over-investing in dimensions already ahead of the constraint

Most orgs are over-invested in models while stuck at Level 2 on governance and adoption — the two dimensions that actually turn capability into shipped value.

Surgical beats comprehensive.

---

## Post 4 — "Next level" beats "best practice"
> Most maturity models fail because they hand you a Level-5 picture and say "go be Google." Useless.

The actionable move is always ONE level up on your constraint.

Governance at Level 2 (ad hoc reviews)? Level 3 is concrete: risk-tiered intake, a written policy, model docs. That's a quarter of work — not a 5-year transformation.

Raise the constraint. The next-weakest dimension becomes the new constraint. Repeat. Maturity is a sequence of single steps, not a leap to the summit.
