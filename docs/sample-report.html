Diagnosis

Northwind's instinct — buy a bigger model platform — would pour money into Data & Infrastructure, already their strongest dimension (Level 4). That's the classic mistake the average hides: a mediocre-looking 2.8 signals "improve everything," when the actual problem is two specific dimensions.
The binding constraint is Governance (Level 2), made high-leverage by their regulated context. In a bank, until there's risk-tiered intake and oversight, nothing can safely reach production — which is exactly why Adoption is also stuck at Level 2. Strong data feeding ungoverned pilots that never ship is the whole problem, in one sentence.

Recommended next move

Raise Governance 2 → 3 first, and pair it with an Adoption push:

1
Stand up risk-tiered intake + AI policy + model documentation — the building blocks of NIST AI RMF's Govern function. See the Governance Playbook for the intake form, risk-tiering workflow, and AI registry schema. This unblocks production, not just compliance.
2
Pick 2–3 stalled pilots and shepherd them through the new governance to production with a measured success metric — moving Adoption 2 → 3. Existing assets, existing funding. No new build required.
3
Do not fund the platform expansion this cycle. Data & Infrastructure is already ahead of the constraint. Revisit once Governance and Adoption catch up to Level 3.
✕
What not to do: Fund a model-platform buildout. It compounds an already-strong dimension while leaving the binding constraint untouched — and does nothing to move pilots out of purgatory. Same budget; very different outcome.
Expected effect: Unblocking the binding constraint converts existing, already-funded data strength into shipped value. Moving Governance 2 → 3 is a focused quarter of work: a risk-tiered intake process, a written AI policy, and model documentation for in-flight systems. That's a smaller investment than the infrastructure expansion it replaces — and the investment that actually unlocks production.
"Are we behind on AI?" produces a platform purchase. "Which dimension is our binding constraint, and what does the next level cost?" produces a governance program that unlocks everything already built. Same budget. Very different outcome. — This is what a maturity model is for: not benchmarking against competitors, but finding the one investment that unlocks everything else.
